**Re: Nomination of Dr Wentao Zhang for the *Electronics* Young Investigator Award 2026**

Dear Members of the Award Committee,

I am writing to nominate **Dr Wentao Zhang** for the *Electronics* Young Investigator Award. I am Professor [Wei Hua] in the School of Electrical Engineering at Southeast University, where my research concerns electrical machines, drives, and their power-electronic control. I have known Dr Zhang since [2018], first as a master's and then as a doctoral researcher in our group, and I have co-authored a number of papers with him. I can therefore speak in detail and at first hand about the quality, independence, and significance of his work.

Dr Zhang's research develops **physics-informed artificial intelligence and validated digital twins for power-electronic and electromechanical systems**. What distinguishes his contribution is that he has connected three layers that are usually treated separately: high-fidelity multiphysics physics, AI-based acceleration, and validation on real hardware. The result is a body of work that turns slow, accurate simulation into fast, differentiable models that can be used in real time. In my assessment this is a genuinely original capability, and an unusually mature one for a researcher at his career stage.

I would draw the Committee's attention to several specific achievements:

- His deep-learning **magnetic-field surrogate** maps design parameters to the full electromagnetic field and reaches R² = 0.9996 for average torque on unseen designs, with more than 94% inference speed-up over finite-element analysis. Because it is differentiable, it can drive gradient-based design directly. This is the computational engine of a real-time digital twin of an electric drive.
- His **validated thermal digital twin** of an oil-cooled machine predicts temperature fields in seconds, validated against dynamometer measurement across the full operating map, where conventional simulation needs many CPU-hours.
- His first-author work received the **2025 IEEE Transactions on Transportation Electrification Prize Paper Award (2nd Prize)**, and in the same year he won **1st Prize in the IEEE TEC Electric Vehicle Design Competition**. He holds **9 patents**, several concerning circuit and control methods for suppressing induced-voltage ripple and torque pulsation in power-electronic drives.
- He has carried this research into practice. Working with Inovance, he designed, built, and tested two high-speed traction drive systems (up to 270 kW, 97.4% efficiency at 20,000 rpm) through the full industrial cycle, acting as technical lead.

His record now comprises 23 journal papers (19 in IEEE Transactions, 11 as first author), 18 conference papers, and 9 patents. For a researcher who completed his PhD in 2025 this is, in my experience, exceptional both in volume and in the consistency of first-author Transactions publications. He also contributes to the community as a reviewer for several IEEE Transactions and as co-chair of a special session at ICEM 2026.

I have supervised and worked alongside many talented young researchers, and I place Dr Zhang among the very best. He combines deep analytical ability with the rarer capacity to build working systems and validate them on hardware, and he does so with independence and integrity. I recommend him for this award **without reservation** and with strong enthusiasm.

I would be glad to provide any further information the Committee may require.
