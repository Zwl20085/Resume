1. (Essential) A PhD in Mechanical Engineering or a closely related discipline, with strong subject knowledge and research expertise relevant to the field.

I hold a PhD in Electrical Engineering, a closely related discipline, from Southeast University (SEU), Nanjing, awarded in September 2025, with the dissertation "Multi-physics Analysis and Design of Hairpin Winding PM Traction Machines in Electric Vehicles". The substance of the dissertation is as much mechanical as electrical: it develops high-resolution lumped-parameter thermal networks and fluid-solid coupled oil-cooling models, mechanical stress and structural-modal analysis of high-speed rotors, and manufacturing-tolerance design of automotive traction machines, validated against AVL dynamometer measurements. My subject expertise sits at the intersection of mechanical, thermal and electrical engineering that defines modern automotive powertrain engineering, which is the field this post addresses.

The mechanical depth of the work is visible in its outputs. My published record includes a high-fidelity oil-cooling thermal model for hairpin-winding machines (IEEE Transactions on Transportation Electrification), field-oriented design of mechanical stress-relieving grooves for high-speed rotors (IEEE IEMDC 2025), and an in-house Python finite-element code package I develop in my current post covering electromagnetic, structural-modal and mechanical-stress analyses. Through a multi-year industrial collaboration with Inovance, a world-leading EV traction-machine supplier, I took two automotive machines from multi-physics design through manufacturing-drawing release and tolerance specification to dynamometer characterisation, gaining working knowledge of stator manufacturing processes (hairpin winding forming, lamination stacking, varnish impregnation). Since September 2025 I have been a Research Assistant at the Power Electronics and Machines Centre (PEMC), University of Nottingham, working on next-generation magnet-free synchronous machines and integrated motor drives within the multi-institution AIMD programme (Nottingham, Bristol, Newcastle).

2. (Essential) A record of high quality research and associated outputs, together with success in securing external research funding or clear potential to do so, informed by a coherent and ambitious research vision in the area of sustainable engineering, particularly with regard to automotive engineering applications.

My research record is built on sustainable automotive engineering: the design of high power-density electrified powertrains for net-zero road transport. I have authored 22 journal papers, 17 in IEEE Transactions (Industrial Electronics, Industry Applications, Transportation Electrification, Energy Conversion), ten as first author, together with 18 peer-reviewed conference papers (IEEE IEMDC, IEEE ECCE, ICEM, ICEMS) and 9 patents. The work has been recognised by the 2025 IEEE TEC Electric Vehicle Design Competition (1st Prize), the 2025 IEEE Transactions on Transportation Electrification Prize Paper Award (2nd Prize), 1st Prize in the IEEE IAS CMD Thesis Contest, the CIEEC Best Paper Award (2023) and the Jiangsu Outstanding Master Thesis Award (2022). These are dynamometer-validated, methodologically complete studies on automotive hardware, and I would aim to sustain that standard of output at Leeds in support of the School's REF submission.

On funding, my doctoral research was carried out within an Inovance-led industrial programme funded under the National Key Research and Development Program of China (Grant No. 2022YFB2502701), through which two EV traction machines were delivered to a production supplier, and my current post sits within the multi-institution AIMD programme across Nottingham, Bristol and Newcastle, which has given me working knowledge of how large collaborative UK programmes are structured, reported and delivered.

My research vision is a coherent programme on AI-driven design and digital twins of electrified powertrains for net-zero road transport, built from three connected strands: multi-physics modelling of automotive traction machines and their converters, coupling electromagnetic, thermal, fluid and mechanical physics; deep-learning surrogate models that replace expensive finite-element evaluations, where my ICEM 2026 paper reaches R-squared = 0.9996 for torque prediction with more than 94% inference speed-up; and experimental validation on dynamometer-tested industrial prototypes. The programme serves sustainability twice over, through vehicles that consume less energy in operation and through magnet-free and reduced-rare-earth machine topologies that lower material criticality, which is the focus of my current AIMD work. The phased funding plan is an EPSRC New Investigator Award within the first eighteen months to establish the surrogate-modelling strand, Innovate UK and Advanced Propulsion Centre industrially co-funded projects building on my prototype delivery record in years two to three, and participation in larger consortium bids (Horizon Europe, Aerospace Technology Institute as the methods extend to electric flight) as the group grows.
