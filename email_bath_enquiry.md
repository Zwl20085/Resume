Dear Dr Graham-Harper-Cater,

I am writing about the Lecturer post in the Department of Electronic & Electrical Engineering (reference SS13726), for which I have submitted an application. The advert is listed with you for informal enquiries, and I wanted to introduce myself briefly because the research directions it names describe my work unusually closely.

My recent work uses deep learning to accelerate the computational workflows behind electrical machine design. In a paper accepted for ICEM 2026, a network with implicit physics-knowledge embedding learns the full magnetic field of an interior PM machine over a 15-dimensional design space, reaching R-squared = 0.9996 for torque prediction with more than 94% inference speed-up over finite-element analysis, running on low-cost GPU hardware. It is built on an in-house Python finite-element package I develop, and I see it as the first step toward something larger: an AI-native simulator for electromagnetic devices that generalises across material topologies, eddy-current and PWM-driven conditions, and is differentiable by construction, so design optimisation and manufacturing-tolerance sensitivity come from gradients rather than parameter sweeps. The models are grounded in hardware: through an industrial collaboration I took two oil-cooled traction machines (18,000 rpm, 113 kW; 20,000 rpm, 270 kW) through the full cycle from multi-physics design to dynamometer characterisation, and I would aim to connect this programme to The Foundry's digital manufacturing agenda.

There is also an existing link to Bath: my PhD supervisor, Prof Zhongze Wu, was a Prize Fellow and Lecturer at IAAPS, and my co-author network includes Bath colleagues from that period.

If it would be useful, I would be glad to have a short call about how this direction fits the Department's plans for computer systems engineering and AI. Thank you for your time.
