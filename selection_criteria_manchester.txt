The responses below address each item of the Person Specification for the Lecturer / Senior Lecturer in Electrical Machines post (SAE-031274). The seven Essential criteria are answered first, followed by the three Strongly Desirable criteria.


1. PhD in Electrical Engineering (Essential)

I hold a PhD in Electrical Engineering from Southeast University (SEU), Nanjing, China (Sep 2021 to Sep 2025), with the dissertation "Multi-physics Analysis and Design of Hairpin Winding PM Traction Machines in Electric Vehicles". My MSc (2018 to 2021) and BEng (2014 to 2018) are also in Electrical Engineering from SEU. My doctoral research, supervised by Prof. Zhongze Wu, focused on the electromagnetic design, advanced thermal modelling, and control of high power-density electrical machines, which align directly with the research focus of this post. The work was recognised by the 2025 IEEE Trans. Transportation Electrification Prize Paper Award (2nd Prize) and the 2025 IEEE TEC Electric Vehicle Design Competition (1st Prize).


2. Industrial Experience including FEA of Electromagnetic Circuits, Machine Design, Machine Prototyping Methods and Laboratory Testing (Essential)

During my PhD I collaborated with Inovance, a world-leading EV traction-machine supplier, on the analysis, design, prototyping, and laboratory testing of two advanced high power-density oil-cooled PM traction machines:

  - 18 000 rpm PM traction machine for A0-class vehicle: measured peak power 113 kW, peak torque 138 Nm, power density 7.78 kW/kg, peak efficiency 95.3%.
  - 20 000 rpm PM traction machine for A-class vehicle: measured peak power 270 kW, peak torque 380 Nm, power density 7.87 kW/kg, peak efficiency 97.4%.

Both projects followed full industrial development cycles. At the design stage, I applied a multi-physics optimisation framework coupling electromagnetic field analysis, thermal modelling, and mechanical stress evaluation. At the manufacturing stage, I produced engineering drawings for the electromagnetic and structural components, gaining substantial experience in tolerance specification and high-reliability manufacturing. At the testing stage, I characterised both prototypes on an advanced AVL dynamometer system, extracting key performance indicators, voltage and current waveforms, and full efficiency maps.

In addition, I have developed an in-house finite element analysis code package covering electromagnetic, structural-modal, and mechanical-stress analyses of electrical machines. This package is now being applied to magnet-free synchronous machine development in my current Research Assistant role at PEMC, University of Nottingham.


3. Outstanding Track Record of Publications (Essential)

I have authored 22 journal papers, including 17 in IEEE Transactions, across IEEE Trans. Industrial Electronics, Industry Applications, Transportation Electrification, and Energy Conversion. I have also presented 18 conference papers at leading international venues including IEEE IEMDC, IEEE ECCE, ICEMS, and ICEM, and hold 9 patents.

The research themes of my publications align directly with the Department's electrical-machines portfolio:

  - Novel machine design and winding technology: novel n-layer flat-wire / hairpin winding design methodology for high power-density EV PM machines, including conductor layout optimisation, mechanical design, and experimental validation.
  - Electromagnetic modelling and analysis: systematic analytical and FEA-based investigation of air-gap field modulation, DC winding-induced voltage, end-effects, and flux regulation in wound-field switched flux machines and hybrid excitation topologies.
  - Advanced thermal management: high-fidelity lumped-parameter thermal network modelling and fluid-solid coupled oil-cooling analysis for hairpin-winding PM machines, validated against prototype measurements.
  - Multi-physics optimisation: mechanism-driven multi-objective optimisation integrating electromagnetic performance, thermal behaviour, and mechanical constraints across multiple machine topologies.
  - Machine control and dynamic modelling: spatial harmonic modelling of machine inductance, DC winding current dynamics under PWM excitation, and converter-machine interaction analysis for wound-field and PM machine systems.

The following responses address the three Strongly Desirable criteria.


(a) Cryogenic Systems

While cryogenic machine design has not been the primary focus of my research, my advanced thermal-modelling expertise provides a rigorous foundation for engaging with cryogenic cooling challenges. In particular, I have developed high-fidelity lumped-parameter thermal networks and coupled fluid-thermal analyses for hairpin-winding PM machines, validated against prototype dynamometer measurements. These modelling techniques are directly transferable to cryogenically cooled machines, where accurate prediction of local hot-spots, conductor temperature gradients, and coolant flow distribution is similarly critical. I am genuinely enthusiastic about developing research capability in this area alongside Manchester's established strengths in superconducting and HTS-machine research, and would welcome the opportunity to contribute to existing or new project proposals in cryogenic and superconducting machines.


(b) Health and Safety in University Laboratories

Throughout my doctoral study at SEU and my Research Assistant role at PEMC Nottingham, I have worked in high-voltage electrical-machine test facilities operating within formal health and safety frameworks, including documented risk assessments, laboratory induction procedures, and safe working procedures for rotating machinery and power-electronics test rigs. I have personally followed lock-out / tag-out protocols on dynamometer test cells, observed permit-to-work procedures during high-voltage battery-emulator operation, and supervised junior students performing measurement campaigns. I appreciate fully the responsibility of managing laboratory environments and equipment for student use, and would commit to completing Manchester's required laboratory-supervisor and risk-assessment training in my first appointment year.


(c) Power Electronics and AC Power Circuit Analysis

A substantial strand of my research addresses the interaction between power-electronic drives and electrical machines, including the effects of PWM excitation on machine losses and temperature, field-winding voltage modulation methods, and induced voltage pulsation. Beyond modelling, I have designed and implemented a three-phase full-bridge converter and associated control algorithm for testing flux-modulation machine prototypes, covering hardware design, gate-drive selection, sensor integration, control-software development on a TI C2000 platform, and laboratory commissioning. This combination of analytical, simulation, and hardware-implementation experience provides a strong basis for contributing to teaching in power electronics and AC power-circuit analysis, and I would welcome the opportunity to complement the Department's existing curriculum in these areas.
