## Notes

1. **Stanford scope.** The programme funds *fundamental research in the natural sciences*. Applicants pick one primary area (astronomy and astrophysics; biological sciences; experimental chemistry; earth and oceans; mathematics and statistics; experimental physics; **theory and simulation**). Engineering is not a listed area, so an application would have to sit under "theory and simulation" and be written as a question about learned operators for nonlinear field problems, with the electrical machine as the test system. Both suggested hosts are engineering faculty, which does not remove that risk. Materials: cover letter, CV, research statement, career statement, three to four references. With 11 days left this is realistic only if the research statement is adapted from the MSCA or Schmidt Toronto text.
2. **Cornell feasibility.** Fifteen days to secure a faculty mentor, an external partner organisation and four letters. The external partner is the hard part and is Wentao's call (an industrial contact would be the natural choice). Research must sit under one of four themes: Climate Risk, **Energy Transitions**, Food Security, One Health. If no mentor reply arrives by about 9 Oct, treat this one as a 2028-cohort target.
3. **Princeton mentor rule.** A faculty member who currently hosts a Distinguished Postdoctoral Fellow cannot be the primary mentor. Ask Prof. Chen about this in the first exchange. Materials: cover letter, CV, 2-page research plan, 1 to 2 page personal statement, 2 to 3 reference letters, plus the mentors' letters.
4. **Email addresses.** Addresses marked "search result" in the drafts came from search summaries of the official pages, not from a page read directly. Open the linked profile and confirm before sending. Three addresses (Kolemen, Rivas-Davila, Onori) were not found and are left blank.
5. **Holding more than one award.** These fellowships, MSCA-PF and the Toronto Schmidt fellowship cannot be held together. Say so plainly if a host asks.
