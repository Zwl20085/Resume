## 5. Framing of the proposed research (lead decision)

Title (working): **Physics-consistent learned models for multiphysics design of electric machines beyond rare-earth magnets**.

Story in one paragraph: electric-machine design is a coupled electromagnetic, thermal and mechanical problem solved by FE at minutes to hours per evaluation; learned surrogates are fast but, as Wentao's own measurements show, a small field error can hide a large energy or loss error, and most surrogates are tied to one topology. The fellowship builds models that are held to the physics they replace (energy-norm and loss-consistent training on his own FE solver), that transfer across topologies and materials (fine-tuning across magnet grades first, then to AMPERE's additively manufactured SynRel and axial-flux designs and rare-earth-lean machines), and that couple to loss and thermal prediction. Every model is checked against measurement, using Wentao's existing dynamometer datasets and AMPERE's hardware.

Three aims over 24 months (keep the plan concrete, with a milestone per aim; no Gantt table needed, a short timeline list is enough):
- Aim 1 (months 1 to 8): physics-consistent field operator. Energy-norm and loss-consistent training; calibrated uncertainty so the FE solver is called only where the model is unsure. Milestone: energy-norm and iron-loss errors within a stated band on held-out designs across at least two topology families.
- Aim 2 (months 6 to 16): transfer to new topologies and materials. Fine-tuning from a frozen checkpoint; test cases: rare-earth-lean and magnet-free machines (AIMD and WFSF background), AMPERE's AM SynRel and axial-flux designs. Milestone: transfer with a stated fraction of the FE data a from-scratch model needs.
- Aim 3 (months 12 to 24): coupled loss-thermal prediction and measurement validation. Loss maps into the validated thermal-network approach [J10]; comparison against dynamometer data; one jointly designed test case with the AMPERE Lab. Milestone: validated multiphysics predictions on one AMPERE machine and one Inovance-derived dataset.
- Resources: GPU compute (the $15,000 allowance covers cloud or workstation GPU time and conference travel); AMPERE Lab machines and test facilities; JMAG (lab sponsor) alongside the in-house solver.

Career goals: a tenure-track faculty post in electrical machines and physics-informed AI, leading a group that designs, builds and tests machines and treats learned models as instruments held to measurement. The fellowship supplies what he does not yet have: an independent programme, a US research network through WEMPEC/GE/ORNL links, formal mentoring of students, grant preparation (NSF, DOE, ARPA-E), and the College's postdoctoral training cohort. Say plainly that he has not yet led a funded project or formally supervised a student.
